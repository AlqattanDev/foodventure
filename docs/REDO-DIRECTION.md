# The saffron tray

Foodventure turns a small souq serving tray into a quick cooking ritual: make one dish with your hands, pass it to a customer, see their delight. The first viewport places original illustrated food inside a sunlit Bahraini stall and offers one large action.

## Direction
Full palette: coral awning, deep teal woodwork, saffron-gold brass, warm ceramic and a pale woven serving cloth. Rounded friendly lettering and original illustrated food share confident, clean silhouettes. The display uses the user's permitted system font option to avoid a font download. The backdrop is an original generated raster; every dish, character and interaction drawing is original code.

## First viewport and path
Open the stall → one unhurried cup of karak on the first visit → a 60-second rush → stars, best score and the next recipe. A row of three customers faces the serving mat. Food stays large enough to read under a thumb. The active recipe has one instruction and one large action; step completion changes the actual food. Serve sends the plate upward, pops the score and makes the customer react.

## Signature interaction
Hold the kettle and the tea visibly rises in the cup; release around its gold fill line. Stirring moves a paddle through glossy halwa or noodles. A swipe folds samboosa. Every gesture has a keyboard/button equivalent. Partial dishes stay with their order when the player chooses another customer.

## Motion and accessibility
Short overshooting food arrivals, drifting steam, controlled score confetti and a distinct day-end celebration. UI transitions 160–260ms. Touch controls at least44px, all actions reachable from keyboard, no hover-only meaning, system reduced-motion respected and mute saved. Hidden tabs pause the day and cancel gestures.

## Design process note
The owner explicitly delegated decisions and requested no questions. Seven grounded systems were considered (souq counter, picnic cloth, tea cart, spice drawers, tiled kitchen window, enamel menu, embroidered feast tray). The design seed selected the feast tray; the remote challenger/reference service was unavailable. This is a code-led game implementation with an original backdrop, not a reproduction of a supplied screen.

## Risks to verify
Touch release and pointer cancellation, short portrait displays, race at the day bell, repeated result handling, old/corrupt/blocked storage, and the deployed relative paths. Midrange physical-device performance remains a separate hardware check.
